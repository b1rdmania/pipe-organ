# The Voicer

The organ was the original robot instrument. This is the next one.

## In front (what people see)

One screen. Two choices. One button.

1. Pick a song.
2. Pick a place.
3. Press build.

The Voicer builds a new organ for that song in that place. You watch it make each pipe, hear each pipe sound wrong, then hear it tune itself until it sings. Then it plays the song. You can save the clip.

That is all. No knobs on the first screen.

## Behind (where the complexity lives)

Each of these is hidden. People feel the result, not the machinery.

| Idea | What it does behind the screen | From |
|---|---|---|
| Voicing loop | Each new pipe adjusts its mouth until its pitch and tone match the target. This is the visible moment. | Fable: THE VOICER |
| A pipe is a small room, a room is a large pipe | The organ and the place are one physical model. The place changes how every pipe speaks. | Fable: altitude |
| The place picks the notes | The room's own resonances choose the key and the voicing, so the organ suits its place. | Fable: SPRINKLE |
| New pipe shapes | For impossible places (the Moon, a whale, a 400 m hall) the Voicer searches for pipe forms nobody has built. | Fable: MUTANT RANK |
| Air you can see | The visual language is the air itself: pressure waves in pipes and room, drawn simply. | Fable: FLATLAND ORGAN |
| The arranger | Bass to pedals, melody and chords across manuals, tempo set by the room's echo. | earlier direction |

## The look

Open. Andy art-directs the Voicer itself, the build sequence and the places. Simple and beautiful in front.

## Out at the edges (later, not v1)

- Real organ finale: the same MIDI on a MIDI-ready pipe organ in a real church.
- ONE EIGHTH: a physical workshop, a 1:8 organ in a box recorded in ultrasound and slowed 8 times.
- MCP so any agent can commission an organ. MIDI and audio out to Ableton.

## The 30-second clip

1. A song title and a place appear. Nothing else.
2. The Voicer cuts the first pipe. It sounds wrong. The mouth moves. It settles into tune.
3. Fast montage: more pipes, each tuning itself, the organ growing into the place.
4. It plays. The place rings.
5. Cut to a different song, a different place, a completely different organ.

## First proof

One song, two places, two organs. Test one thing: does watching a pipe tune itself, then hearing the whole organ in its place, give a chill? If not, stop.
